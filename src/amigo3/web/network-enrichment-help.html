What is tested?

Neighborhood enrichment asks: does this gene list have more connections to a fixed local neighborhood than equally connected random gene lists do? A connection can come from a study gene inside or outside the neighborhood. This tests connectivity, rather than simply counting how many study genes belong to the neighborhood.

The targets are overlapping sets of genes discovered from network structure. The biological process (BP) names beside them describe their members; they are not the terms being tested. The separate GO-term connectivity (GO-CAM) method uses GO-annotated gene sets as targets instead. Ordinary GO overrepresentation tests gene membership rather than network edges.

Building the network

For the selected taxon, AmiGO combines explicit causal links between activities in GO-CAMs. Each activity’s gene-product enabler becomes a node; a causal assertion between two activities becomes a gene pair. Shared genes join models together.

The analysis uses one undirected, unweighted connection per distinct pair. Repeated assertions do not add weight. It does not add connections just because genes occur in the same model, expand complexes, or infer transitive connections. Original directions, relation types, evidence and source models remain available in the pair inspector.

These are functional connections, not necessarily physical protein interactions. For example, one enzyme can provide the input for another. The combined network also spans biological contexts; its edges need not coexist in one cell.

Finding local neighborhoods

Neighborhoods are constructed from the reference network before looking at your study genes or their GO annotations. They are bounded local regions, rather than entire connected components or a partition into disjoint communities.

  1. Start at every gene. A personalized PageRank walk spreads from one seed at a time, repeatedly returning to that seed so nearby genes receive more weight.
  2. Rank nearby genes. Divide each gene’s PageRank score by its degree (number of neighbors), then examine connected prefixes of that ranking containing the seed.
  3. Choose a local boundary. Among prefixes of 2–50 genes, choose the lowest conductance: the number of edges leaving the set divided by the smaller of the degree sums inside and outside it, within the seed’s connected component. Lower conductance means a more separated boundary.
  4. Combine identical sets. Keep all their generating seeds. Distinct sets may overlap and remain separate enrichment targets.

Why does CHEK1–CDC25A–CDK1 appear more than once?

Different seeds can identify overlapping neighborhoods around the same mechanism. Seeing a motif in several neighborhoods is expected; it is not independent evidence several times over. The overview links show this shared membership.

Seeds identify where discovery began. Conductance describes the reference boundary, not enrichment significance. BP labels are added afterward: up to three positive, propagated BP annotation sets with at least two shared members, ranked by Jaccard overlap. They do not influence neighborhood discovery or scores.

Exact construction settings

Policy gocam-local-pagerank-v1 uses a lazy personalized PageRank walk with restart probability 0.15, residual/degree tolerance 0.00001 and deterministic FIFO residual pushes. The stationary equation is p = 0.15 × seed + 0.85 × p × (I + D⁻¹A)/2. Rank by p/degree, breaking ties by gene identifier. Conductance ties within 10⁻¹² favor the smaller prefix.

A whole component is eligible only if it fits the 50-gene cap and no proper eligible prefix has conductance below 0.5. If no prefix is eligible, use the seed and its first neighbor and report the fallback. This is a bounded local PageRank heuristic inspired by Andersen, Chung and Lang, not the full PageRank-Nibble algorithm or its guarantees.

Scores and significance

Observed connections count distinct pairs joining your study set to the target. A pair with both endpoints in both sets still counts once. Direct membership alone contributes no connection.

Expected connections come from random gene sets with the same size and exact degree histogram as your eligible study set. For example, if your study includes three genes with degree four, each comparison set includes three degree-four genes. The graph and target neighborhoods stay fixed. Expected counts are calculated analytically under this model; fold enrichment is observed divided by expected.

P-values compare the observed count with those random sets. “Enriched connectivity” uses the upper tail; “Depleted connectivity” uses the lower tail, including ties. If all possible comparison sets fit within the requested count, they are enumerated. Otherwise, independent random sets are drawn with replacement using the chosen seed.

With random draws, p = (extreme counts + 1)/(draws + 1). The default 1,999 draws has a minimum p-value of 0.0005. More draws improve resolution and take longer. Exhaustive tests use the fraction of all possible sets that are at least as extreme.

BY FDR is the displayed Benjamini–Yekutieli adjusted p-value, accounting for dependent tests such as overlapping neighborhoods. Correction includes every eligible tested neighborhood, including zero-connection results, before display filtering or result truncation. A small raw p-value or large fold change can therefore have a large adjusted value.

Interesting to inspect does not mean statistically significant

In the G2M example on build 48879e043488a9a3, N178 contains 7 study genes among 44 members. It has 30 connections versus about 9.74 expected, with p = 0.0005 but BY FDR ≈ 0.666 (1,999 random sets, seed 0, default size bounds). It is useful to inspect, but does not pass BY FDR ≤ 0.05.

Analysis settings and display filters

Analysis settings change what is tested. A custom background induces a smaller graph, removes isolated genes and intersects the fixed reference neighborhoods with the remaining genes. Min/max genes per neighborhood then filter those memberships. This can disconnect a neighborhood; it does not rediscover its reference boundary.

Overview filters only change what you see. By default, the overview shows the top 20 returned neighborhoods with at least two study members, ranked by lowest p-value. It includes nonsignificant results for exploration.

Min/max genes per neighborhood
Limits the number of background members in each tested set. Max 10 excludes a 44-gene neighborhood. The construction cap remains 50.
Show top
Limits the number of circles displayed. To see ten neighborhoods, including larger sets, choose Show top 10 and leave the analysis size bounds unchanged.
Minimum study members
Requires direct study membership, regardless of the number of connecting edges. The default of 2 hides 1/N neighborhoods.
Rank by
Orders eligible returned results by p-value, study count, study fraction or fold enrichment. It does not change their scores.
Apply BY FDR
Restricts the overview to the current threshold. It starts off; editing the main BY FDR threshold also enables it. If nothing passes, the overview stays empty.
Show all returned table/chart rows
Controls the table and count chart separately. It does not override the overview’s filters.
Expand neighborhood
Includes all returned neighborhoods, even those hidden in the overview. Selecting one does not add a circle that fails the overview filters.

Overview ranking is limited to returned results; its status reports any result truncation. Changing these display controls never reduces the multiple-testing correction family.

Reading the two graph views

Overview: each circle is a neighborhood

Circle area reflects the number of study members. Labels such as 7/44 mean seven study genes among 44 members in the analysis background, followed by a representative BP label (or seed names). A green outline marks results passing the current BY FDR threshold.

Lines show shared genes between neighborhoods, not interactions between them. Circles are arranged by the chosen rank for readability; distance is not a biological similarity measure. Select a circle to inspect its gene network below.

Detail: each node is a gene

Blue circles are study-only genes; green squares are neighborhood-only genes; orange diamonds belong to both. The initial view shows counted study-to-neighborhood pairs, so it can include study genes outside the neighborhood and omit members that have no counted connection.

Solid lines contribute to the score; dashed lines are context only. “Show full neighborhood context” adds all background members and internal edges. It changes the drawing, while the score always tests the full eligible neighborhood. The counted-pair view can look disconnected even when the reference neighborhood is connected.

Select a gene or pair to inspect its sources. Pair details retain the original directed activity assertions, evidence and model links. Drag nodes to arrange them, drag the background to pan, scroll to zoom, and use Fit graph or Save PNG.

Limits and reproducibility

The network reflects incomplete, context-dependent GO-CAM curation. A local boundary is a useful hypothesis, not an established pathway. Hub-centered neighborhoods can change with locality settings. Exact degree matching can leave some study genes fixed in every comparison set and reduce power; it does not remove all study-design or curation biases. BY correction and finite random-sampling resolution can also limit significance.

We previously tried disjoint greedy modularity communities. In the G2M example this grouped the checkpoint motif into a large GPCR-heavy community through connecting chains. Overlapping local PageRank neighborhoods replaced that approach to retain smaller, inspectable regions.

Use Copy link to retain the analysis settings, data build, selected neighborhood and display controls. Download TSV records construction metadata, counts, scores and descriptive annotation context. Neighborhood numbers can change between builds; GN: identifiers derive from the taxon and reference membership.
